# Privacy Policy

**Last updated: August 19, 2026**

## The Short Version

Reflections needs some of your information to work.

We collect what we need.  
We use it to provide the Service.  
We don't sell your personal information.  
Private reflections stay private according to your selected visibility.  
You can request deletion.

**That's the policy. Everything below explains the nouns and verbs.**

---

## 1. What We Collect

When you use Reflections, we may collect:

- account details such as name, username, email, and profile image;
- your C.H.A.T. reflections, notes, titles, and Bible selections;
- AI conversations;
- posts, comments, reactions, and community activity;
- preferences and support requests; and
- ordinary technical information such as IP address, browser, device, security events, and error logs.

We use this information to provide, secure, maintain, and improve Reflections.

## 2. Login Providers

You may be able to sign in through services such as Facebook or Google.

They may provide information you authorize, such as your account identifier, name, email address, and profile image. We use it to authenticate you and operate your account.

**We do not receive your Facebook or Google password through normal OAuth authentication.**

We don't want it.

Facebook doesn't want us to have it.

Our database doesn't want it.

Our developer **really** doesn't want it.

Please let this beautiful alignment of interests continue.

Third-party login providers process information under their own terms and privacy policies.

---

## 3. Private Means Private

Reflections may let you designate content as **Private**, **Community**, or **Public**.

We use your selected visibility to control access.

Creating a reflection does not make it public.

Sharing it publicly does.

This distinction has somehow required billions of dollars of software engineering across human civilization, but **we intend to respect it.**

If you intentionally share content, people who can see it may copy, quote, screenshot, or remember it. We cannot remotely erase someone's memory.

**That feature remains on the roadmap immediately after teleportation.**

---

## 4. AI

If you use AI features, relevant content may be sent to an AI provider so the AI can answer you.

This can include your prompt, relevant conversation context, and information required by the feature.

Do not put passwords, API keys, credit-card numbers, government identifiers, or other secrets into AI conversations.

### AI does not need:

`AWS_SECRET_ACCESS_KEY=...`

to discuss **Psalm 23**.

If it asks, we have considerably larger problems.

Third-party AI providers process information according to the agreements and privacy terms governing those services.

---

## 5. Bible Providers

We may use third-party Bible services to retrieve Scripture.

They may receive information necessary to fulfill the request, such as the translation and passage requested.

If you request John 3:16, the Bible service may need:

**John 3:16**

It does not inherently need:

**John 3:16 + your complete spiritual history + what Kevin did + why you are still annoyed about it.**

This principle has an official name:

**Data minimization.**

We prefer:

**Mind thine own database.**

## 16. The Berean Rule

Here's the biblical privacy principle we actually like.

**Acts 17:11:** the Bereans examined the Scriptures to verify what **Paul and Silas** were teaching.

Think about the confidence level required here.

**Paul:** Here's the message.

**Bereans:** Interesting. We'll check.

**Paul:** Fair enough.

So please don't believe us merely because this page says:

### YOUR PRIVACY IS IMPORTANT TO US™

Check.

Look at the permissions Reflections requests.

Ask why we need them.

Check whether private content actually behaves as private content.

And **1 Thessalonians 5:21** gives the wonderfully compact version:

**Test everything. Hold fast what is good.**

If the Bereans could check Paul, **you can check our app.**

If Reflections behaves contrary to this Policy, don't reinterpret the Policy until the bug disappears.

**Tell us. We fix the app.**
